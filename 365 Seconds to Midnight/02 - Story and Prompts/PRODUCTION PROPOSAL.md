# 365 Seconds to Midnight — Production Proposal

**Version:** 1.0 · **Status:** Awaiting user approval · **Flow project:** https://flow.google.com/project/e417e320-1ef8-4b73-9452-ac2a13d714a4

## Project brief

An original South Korean high-society time-loop murder-mystery micro-drama for English-speaking viewers. The short-form deliverable is a six-episode vertical season for TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels. All dialogue and captions are in English. Seoul is the story setting; no character accent is inferred from nationality or appearance. Violence is stylized and non-graphic; the emphasis is suspense, betrayal, and deduction.

**Approved story:** *365 Seconds to Midnight* (자정까지 365초). Shin Woo-jin relives the last 365 seconds before midnight after being poisoned at his engagement party. He and undercover journalist Yoon Seo-ah use information from failed loops to expose the hired killers and their syndicate employer. The last successful loop ends at 12:00:01 AM with the mastermind exposed and arrested.

**Clock continuity:** 11:53:55 PM to 12:00:00 AM is exactly 365 seconds. The opening preview shows Woo-jin's betrayal and collapse at 11:59:59; the first reset then reveals the full 365-second window. In each failed loop he returns to 11:53:55. The successful final loop crosses midnight.

## Recommended creative treatment

- **Format and audience:** six 30-second vertical episodes, each with a hook, one clear escalation, and a final reveal or question. The first season completes a single story arc.
- **Visual style:** original glossy cinematic thriller: restrained gold and black in the penthouse, ruby-red wine as a recurring clue, cool steel-blue in the cellar/server room, silver highlights on Hee-joo. Avoid imitating a specific filmmaker or studio.
- **Camera/edit:** establish geography in clean symmetrical wides; use controlled push-ins for suspicion, clock/watch inserts to track seconds, and repeat matched framing at each reset. Use handheld movement sparingly during attacks. The final loop reuses earlier compositions with small changed details so the audience can compare what Woo-jin learned.
- **Lighting:** warm party practicals and fireworks reflections in the ballroom; cooler, harder security lighting below. Keep screen graphics simple and legible, with the countdown added or corrected in editing if Flow cannot render exact numerals.
- **Sound:** original low pulse under the countdown, glass clink, room tone, muffled party music, watch tick, and a distinctive short rewind/impact cue at resets. Dialogue remains intelligible; music ducks under lines. Avoid recognizable copyrighted songs.
- **Platform framing:** 9:16; faces, wine glass, and countdown remain inside central safe area. Prepare platform-specific captions and covers after the final edit. Use fresh publishing-rule checks later.

## Season beats and dialogue targets

Target episode duration: 30 seconds each. Each episode is planned as three short Flow clips, approximately 10 seconds apiece, then edited to the target. Actual model duration limits and costs must be checked in Flow before video prompts are prepared. These are timing targets, not a claim of frame-accurate generative control.

### Episode 1 — “The First Midnight”

**Logline/cliffhanger:** Woo-jin dies at the engagement party, then wakes six minutes earlier with the same glass in his hand. Cliffhanger: his watch displays 365 seconds.

- **0:00–0:04:** Champagne, fireworks and a fast visual of Hee-joo kissing the CFO as Woo-jin collapses. Dialogue (Woo-jin, breathless): “Hee-joo…?”
- **0:04–0:15:** Match cut to Woo-jin upright at the same table, glass untouched. He checks his watch. Dialogue (Woo-jin): “No. I was just—”
- **0:15–0:30:** Countdown resets to 06:05 remaining; Woo-jin looks up and sees the same guests in their earlier positions. Dialogue (Woo-jin, low): “Three hundred sixty-five seconds.” Final insert: the glass trembles in his hand.

### Episode 2 — “Wrong Suspect”

**Logline/cliffhanger:** Woo-jin confronts his fiancée and learns someone else is watching him. Cliffhanger: a red targeting dot appears on the ballroom glass.

- **0:00–0:10:** Woo-jin refuses the wine and corners Hee-joo. Dialogue (Woo-jin): “What did you put in my glass?” Dialogue (Hee-joo): “You’re frightening me.”
- **0:10–0:20:** He notices the CFO signaling across the room. He turns toward the window; a red dot lands on his lapel.
- **0:20–0:30:** Seo-ah sees the dot and knocks him down as the shot hits the window. Dialogue (Seo-ah): “Down!” End on Woo-jin recognizing her.

### Episode 3 — “Every Exit”

**Logline/cliffhanger:** Woo-jin changes tactics, but the room has more than one planned attack. Cliffhanger: security seals the ballroom doors.

- **0:00–0:10:** Reset; Woo-jin switches glasses and tracks the CFO’s hand. Dialogue (Woo-jin): “Let’s see who drinks first.”
- **0:10–0:20:** A sharp metallic snap overhead; guests look up as the chandelier begins to fall. Keep impact offscreen and non-graphic.
- **0:20–0:30:** Reset; Woo-jin looks toward the security chief, who silently locks the doors. Dialogue (Woo-jin): “You too?”

### Episode 4 — “The Room Is Bought”

**Logline/cliffhanger:** Woo-jin realizes the attackers are coordinated and finds the one person who has been documenting the party. Cliffhanger: Seo-ah reveals she has footage from earlier loops.

- **0:00–0:10:** Woo-jin lists each conspirator’s position and glances at the stepmother. Dialogue (Woo-jin): “You hired all of them.”
- **0:10–0:20:** Seo-ah leads him through the service corridor toward the wine cellar/server room. Dialogue (Seo-ah): “I’m press. I came for the contract—not your murder.”
- **0:20–0:30:** In the cool-lit server room, she shows the button-camera. Dialogue (Seo-ah): “I remember the first time you died.”

### Episode 5 — “One Honest Witness”

**Logline/cliffhanger:** Seo-ah and Woo-jin compare what they know and identify the syndicate’s mastermind. Cliffhanger: the mastermind is already in the room.

- **0:00–0:10:** They map the VIP room from memory; close inserts on watch, camera, and party plan. Dialogue (Woo-jin): “Every time I stop one, another starts.”
- **0:10–0:20:** Seo-ah studies a reflection in the server monitor. Dialogue (Seo-ah): “Then we let them reveal each other.”
- **0:20–0:30:** Monitor reflection identifies the stepmother speaking to the rival syndicate’s unseen handler. Dialogue (Seo-ah): “Your stepmother isn’t the boss.”

### Episode 6 — “One Second After”

**Logline/cliffhanger/payoff:** In the final loop, Woo-jin and Seo-ah use precise timing and the conspirators’ conflicting plans to expose the mastermind. Cliffhanger resolves: the countdown passes midnight and police enter.

- **0:00–0:10:** Reset; Woo-jin and Seo-ah move to their agreed positions. Dialogue (Woo-jin): “When the clock hits twelve, show them the footage.”
- **0:10–0:20:** Across the room, each conspirator’s cover story collapses as another’s recorded evidence appears on the venue display. No detailed weapon or sabotage mechanics; suspense comes from reactions and timing.
- **0:20–0:30:** Countdown reaches 00:00:01; sirens and police lights fill the glass. Dialogue (Seo-ah): “It’s over.” Dialogue (Woo-jin): “One second after.” End on the watch at 12:00:01.

Dialogue lengths are intentionally sparse to allow reaction, music and visual storytelling at 30 seconds. Rehearse timing in the edit; shorten or redistribute only with approval if actual clips cannot accommodate the lines naturally.

## Locations and prop continuity

1. **Sky-lounge penthouse ballroom:** rectangular glass-walled room with the Lotte World Tower visible beyond; digital countdown centered on the main wall; party table and wine service at camera-left; windows camera-right; service-corridor doors at rear. Gold balloons and warm practicals; guests maintain the same positions across resets.
2. **VIP wine cellar:** narrow, glass-walled room below the ballroom, cool steel-blue light, wine racks along both sides, one central work surface, visible single entrance. Keep its geography clear and movement physically plausible.
3. **Security server room:** distinct small room adjoining the service corridor; monitor wall, console and one door. Do not merge its layout with the cellar.

Key props: identical ruby-red wine glasses and decanter (track which hand holds each); Woo-jin’s left-wrist chronograph; Seo-ah’s concealed button-camera and discreet earpiece; Hee-joo’s engagement ring; stepmother’s gold brooch; security chief’s comms earpiece; large digital countdown display. Avoid readable small device text; create exact countdown numerals in post if needed. No real-world technical details for poison, firearms, explosives, or security bypasses appear in prompts.
